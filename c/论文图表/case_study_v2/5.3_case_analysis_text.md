# 5.3 Case Analysis

We now employ a counterfactual approach to analyze extreme cases observed during the competition. Specifically, we combine our model's predicted fan voting results with the actual judges' scores to determine if the rankings of four renowned celebrities would shift under different scoring rules. Furthermore, we simulate the introduction of a "Judges' Save" (where judges eliminate the two lowest-scored contestants) mechanism to observe its impact on their final rankings.

For each case, we present a comprehensive four-panel dashboard (Figure X) that illustrates:
- **(A) Rank Divergence Over Time**: The trajectory of judge ranking versus estimated fan ranking across competition weeks, with the shaded area highlighting the divergence zone.
- **(B) Final Week Snapshot**: A scatter plot with heatmap background showing the position of each contestant in the Judge Score × Fan Vote space during the critical final week.
- **(C) Fan Vote Distribution**: MCMC-estimated posterior distributions of fan vote shares, visualized as violin plots.
- **(D) Counterfactual Outcome Analysis**: Bar chart comparing the contestant's placement under the actual system, an alternative scoring method, and with the Judges' Save mechanism.

---

## 5.3.1 The Case of Jerry Rice (Season 2)

In Season 2, which utilized the Rank-based scoring method, NFL legend Jerry Rice finished as the runner-up despite receiving the second-lowest scores from the judges.

**Panel (A) Analysis**: The rank divergence plot reveals a consistent gap between Rice's judge ranking (blue line, hovering around rank 4-5) and his fan ranking (red line, consistently at rank 1-2). The purple shaded area quantifies this divergence, showing that Rice maintained a 3-4 rank advantage in fan voting throughout most of the competition.

**Panel (B) Analysis**: The final week snapshot positions Rice in the "Low Score, High Popularity" quadrant (upper-left region of the heatmap). While Drew Lachey achieved the highest combined position, Rice's substantial fan vote share compensated for his technical shortcomings.

Rice's survival can be attributed to the specific mechanics of the Rank system when the contestant pool shrinks significantly. When Rice received the lowest judge scores, only five contestants remained. At this stage, the weight of fan voting within an ordinal ranking system increases significantly. Even if ranked low by judges, as long as Rice secured the top spot in fan voting, his combined rank sum would be 6. This score is sufficient to compete with contestants who ranked at the bottom of the fan vote, effectively offsetting the judges' low scores.

**Panel (D) Counterfactual Results**:
- **Current System (Rank-based)**: 2nd Place
- **Alternative System (Percentage-based)**: ≤2nd Place — The percentage system would have actually amplified Rice's advantage, as his estimated 30%+ fan vote share would translate directly into score weight.
- **With Judges' Save**: Eliminated in Week 7 — Under the Rank-based system combined with Judges' Save, Rice would have been eliminated when judges were given discretion over the bottom two.

## 5.3.2 The Case of Billy Ray Cyrus (Season 4)

Country singer Billy Ray Cyrus finished fifth in Season 4 under the Percentage-based system. Our model's prediction for his fan voting distribution reveals his distinct advantage in fan support volume.

**Panel (A) Analysis**: The divergence plot shows Cyrus's judge ranking (blue) fluctuating between ranks 4-8, while his fan ranking (red) steadily improved from rank 3 to rank 1 by mid-season. The maximum divergence gap reached 7 ranks in Week 5, when Cyrus ranked last among judges but first in fan voting.

**Panel (B) Analysis**: In the Week 8 snapshot, Cyrus occupies a position with moderate judge score (~6.3) but relatively low fan vote share (0.06), suggesting his elimination was imminent as fan support waned.

The data shows Cyrus held a distinct advantage in the volume of fan votes during his peak weeks. Under the percentage system, this numerical advantage was sufficient to mitigate his technical flaws in dance.

**Panel (D) Counterfactual Results**:
- **Current System (Percentage-based)**: 5th Place
- **Alternative System (Rank-based)**: Eliminated in Week 6 — The Rank system converts "overwhelming popularity" into a simple ordinal number, which removes the surplus of his votes.
- **With Judges' Save**: Eliminated in Week 6 — Under either system with Judges' Save, Cyrus would not have survived past Week 6 as judges would have exercised their discretion.

## 5.3.3 The Case of Bristol Palin (Season 11)

In Season 11 (which utilized the percentage-based method), Bristol Palin defeated Brandy despite her judges' scores lagging behind consistently.

**Panel (A) Analysis**: Palin's trajectory shows a dramatic divergence pattern. Her judge ranking (blue) started at rank 10 and gradually improved to rank 4, while her fan ranking (red) remained at rank 1-2 throughout most weeks. The shaded divergence zone reveals gaps of 5-8 ranks during the early-to-mid competition phases.

**Panel (B) Analysis**: The final week heatmap positions Palin in a moderately contested zone. While Jennifer Grey dominated with both high judge score (10.0) and high fan vote (0.52), Palin's estimated fan share was sufficient to secure third place ahead of Brandy, whose technical excellence (9.5) was undermined by limited fan support (0.06).

The model reveals that Palin received substantial fan votes throughout the competition. This volume advantage was likely the decisive factor in overcoming Brandy's higher technical scores.

**Panel (D) Counterfactual Results**:
- **Current System (Percentage-based)**: 3rd Place
- **Alternative System (Rank-based)**: ~3rd Place — By this stage of the competition, the number of contestants was highly compressed. Since our model indicates Palin's fan ranking was still superior to Brandy's, simply switching the scoring method would not have been enough to change the outcome.
- **With Judges' Save**: Eliminated in Week 11 — Under the Rank-based system, the Judges' Save mechanism would have led to her elimination as judges would have favored Brandy's superior technical skills.

## 5.3.4 The Case of Bobby Bones (Season 27)

In Season 27, Bobby Bones won the championship despite consistent low judges' scores. This case represents the most extreme example of fan voting dominance.

**Panel (A) Analysis**: The divergence plot for Bobby Bones shows the most striking pattern among all four cases. His judge ranking (blue) consistently hovered between ranks 6-10 (frequently in the bottom third), while his fan ranking (red) maintained a perfect rank 1 throughout all nine weeks of competition. The divergence gap ranged from 3 to 9 ranks, demonstrating unprecedented fan loyalty.

**Panel (B) Analysis**: The final week heatmap reveals Bobby Bones's exceptional position: despite a judge score of 9.0 (lower than competitors Milo Manheim and Evanna Lynch who both scored 10.0), his estimated fan vote share of 54% dominated the field. The heatmap's intensity around his position reflects this outlier status.

Apparently, Bobby Bones possesses a formidable advantage in fan votes, driven by his massive radio audience following.

**Panel (D) Counterfactual Results**:
- **Current System (Percentage-based)**: Champion (1st Place)
- **Alternative System (Rank-based)**: Champion — Even under the Rank system, Bobby Bones would still win the championship because his fan ranking remained #1 consistently.
- **With Judges' Save**: Champion — The fan voting advantage of Bobby Bones is so powerful that even the mechanism of Judges' Save cannot stop him from winning the championship. This is because he never entered the bottom two, completely bypassing the Save mechanism.

**Critical Insight**: The Bobby Bones case exposes a fundamental limitation of the Judges' Save mechanism — it only activates when a contestant enters the bottom two. Contestants with overwhelming fan support may **never** enter the danger zone, rendering the mechanism entirely ineffective.

# 5.4 Conclusion

In light of these findings, we strongly recommend that future tournaments consistently adopt the rank-based system supplemented by the judge elimination mechanism. The rank-based system effectively mitigates the disproportionate impact of extreme fan voting imbalances. Furthermore, our simulation of the four extreme cases confirms that the judge elimination mechanism serves as an efficient filter, proactively screening out contestants who lack sufficient technical proficiency at an early stage.

**Summary Table: Counterfactual Analysis Results**

| Case | Actual System | Actual Result | Alternative System | With Judges' Save |
|------|---------------|---------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Jerry Rice | Rank | 2nd Place | ≤2nd (benefit) | Eliminated W7 |
| Billy Ray Cyrus | Percentage | 5th Place | Eliminated W6 | Eliminated W6 |
| Bristol Palin | Percentage | 3rd Place | ~3rd (similar) | Eliminated W11 |
| Bobby Bones | Percentage | Champion | Champion | Champion |

**Key Takeaways**:
1. **3 of 4 controversy cases occurred under the Percentage system** — suggesting it is more susceptible to fan-dominated outcomes.
2. **The Rank system would have changed outcomes for Billy Ray Cyrus** — converting his vote surplus into mere ordinal ranking.
3. **Judges' Save is effective but has a bypass vulnerability** — as demonstrated by Bobby Bones, who never triggered the mechanism.
4. **We recommend a "Technical Threshold Rule"** — if a contestant ranks lowest in judge scores for 2+ consecutive weeks, they should automatically enter the bottom two for judge review, regardless of fan vote totals.
